About the Workshop

AIRCom 2027 brings together researchers, language practitioners, community representatives, and technology developers to advance AI that serves linguistically diverse and under-resourced communities.

Rapid advances in AI have expanded the possibilities for language technology. Yet Indigenous, endangered, minority, and other under-resourced languages remain inadequately represented in widely used datasets, models, and benchmarks. Many communities therefore still lack reliable, culturally appropriate tools for communication, education, healthcare, and public services.

AIRCom focuses on multilingual natural language processing, speech and multimodal technologies, foundation models, participatory data creation, community-informed evaluation, and responsible deployment. We welcome technical innovations as well as community-led and interdisciplinary perspectives that respect local expertise, language rights, and data stewardship.

Topics of Interest

We invite original research, resources, benchmarks, system descriptions, interdisciplinary studies, and position papers. Topics include, but are not limited to:

Language & Speech Technologies

  • Machine translation, automatic speech recognition, speech synthesis, and spoken language understanding
  • Language identification, transliteration, OCR, and document understanding
  • Dialectal, code-mixed, and morphology-aware NLP
  • Language documentation, preservation, and revitalization

Multilingual & Foundation Models

  • Multilingual language models and resource-efficient LLMs
  • Cross-lingual transfer, few-shot and zero-shot learning, and low-resource adaptation
  • Tokenization, continued pretraining, and parameter-efficient fine-tuning
  • Retrieval-augmented generation, dictionary-guided and grounded systems

Multimodal & Accessible AI

  • Speech–text, vision–language, and audio-visual learning
  • Multimodal systems for oral-first and low-literacy settings
  • Accessible interfaces for diverse user communities
  • Offline, on-device, and computationally efficient AI

Community-Centered Data & Resources

  • Participatory data collection, annotation, and resource development
  • Native-speaker expertise and community-led validation
  • Corpora, linguistic resources, and culturally grounded benchmarks
  • Community data governance, informed consent, attribution, and language rights

Evaluation & Responsible AI

  • Community-informed benchmarks and human evaluation
  • Linguistic fidelity, dialect coverage, and cultural representation
  • Fairness, robustness, uncertainty, hallucination, and domain shift
  • Reproducibility, error analysis, negative results, and evaluation beyond automatic metrics

Applications & Community Impact

  • Mother-tongue education, literacy, accessibility, and healthcare information
  • Agriculture, rural livelihoods, and access to public services
  • Cultural heritage, climate resilience, and civic participation
  • Participatory design, field studies, and sustainable deployment

We particularly encourage research developed with or led by language communities, and work addressing minority languages, dialects, code-mixed varieties, and resource-constrained environments.

Call for Papers & Submission Guidelines

AIRCom welcomes both archival papers reporting original unpublished work and non-archival presentations intended for research exchange.

Archival Research Papers

  • Long papers: up to 8 pages of main content.
  • Short papers: up to 4 pages of main content.

References are excluded. Accepted archival papers may use one additional page of main content in the camera-ready version, subject to the adopted ACL guidelines.

Accepted papers are intended for publication in the official workshop proceedings, subject to final conference requirements.

Non-Archival Presentations

  • Extended abstract: up to 2 pages, excluding references.
  • Previously published or accepted work, work in progress, systems, datasets, and community projects are welcome.

Non-archival presentations will not be published in the workshop proceedings. Authors must respect other venues’ and publishers’ policies.

Submissions should use the official ACL style and follow applicable policies on anonymization, originality, citation, ethics, and simultaneous submission. Archival papers will be peer-reviewed for relevance, technical soundness, originality, evidence, and clarity.

Submission portal: The AIRCom OpenReview link will be added when available. The pre-reviewed ACL Rolling Review (ARR) commitment route is subject to final workshop arrangements. Please do not submit through another workshop’s portal.

See the ACL/ARR author guidelines for general submission policies. Workshop-specific instructions will be posted here when confirmed.

  • Invited talks on multilingual, low-resource, and responsible AI
  • Research presentations of accepted archival papers
  • Poster sessions and system or dataset demonstrations
  • Community perspectives and discussions

Mentorship & Community Participation

We aim to support students, early-career researchers, independent contributors, language practitioners, and researchers from linguistically or geographically underrepresented communities. Possible activities include pre-submission guidance, small-group discussions, research feedback, and conversations with experienced researchers and community experts.

We welcome perspectives from educators, annotators, local organizations, and community representatives; prior academic publication is not required to contribute to non-archival discussions or demonstrations. Detailed mentorship arrangements will be announced.
